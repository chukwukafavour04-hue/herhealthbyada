02 / KNOW THE SIGNS

Awareness starts with noticing.

Symptoms vary, and early cancer may cause no noticeable symptoms. Keep up with recommended screening and seek care for changes that are new, unusual, persistent, or worrying.

01
♡

Breast cancer

Look for a new lump or thickening, a change in breast size or shape, skin dimpling, nipple changes, or unusual discharge.

Cervical cancer

Possible warning signs include unusual bleeding or discharge and pelvic pain. Screening and HPV vaccination can help prevent cervical cancer.

Ovarian cancer

Persistent bloating, abdominal or pelvic pain, feeling full quickly, or needing to urinate more often should be discussed with a clinician.

Endometrial cancer

Unusual vaginal bleeding — especially after menopause — is important to get checked promptly, even if it happens only once.

03 / TAKE YOUR NEXT STEP

You deserve answers, not guesswork.

If you notice a new or persistent change, arrange an appointment with a qualified healthcare professional. Describe when it started, how often it happens, and what makes it better or worse.

  • ✓ Write down your symptoms and when they began.
  • ✓ Ask what examination, screening, or tests may be appropriate.
  • ✓ Keep follow-up appointments and ask questions if something is unclear.
+

When to seek urgent help

Seek urgent medical care for severe or rapidly worsening symptoms, heavy bleeding, fainting, difficulty breathing, or severe abdominal/pelvic pain. If you think it is an emergency, use your local emergency service.

Do not wait for this tool to advise you if you feel seriously unwell.

PREVENTION & SCREENING

Screening is personal.

Ask a healthcare professional which screenings are appropriate for your age, medical history, family history, and local guidelines. A symptom check is not a screening test.
